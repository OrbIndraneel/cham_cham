Comprehensive Historical Report & Dataset Analysis of Natural Disasters in India (1950–2026)

An Expanded 76-Year Machine Learning Feature Engineering Reference for Spatial-Temporal Prediction

1. Executive Summary & ML Dataset Architecture

This report provides an exhaustive, structured inventory of major natural disasters recorded across the Republic of India from August 1950 through early 2026. Expanding the dataset baseline back to 1950 incorporates critical mid-century benchmark events, including massive intraplate/subduction earthquakes, historical severe droughts, and major cyclone disasters prior to modern satellite tracking.

The primary objective of this document is to serve as an expanded, long-horizon grounding dataset for Synthetic Data Generation (SDG) and Predictive Machine Learning (ML) modeling. Capturing a 76-year observation window allows models to learn long-term multi-decadal oscillations (e.g., ENSO, Indian Ocean Dipole), tectonic strain accumulation cycles, and climate-shifted frequency acceleration.

2. Spatial-Temporal Vulnerability & Common Predictive Factors

Over the 1950–2026 observation period, clear spatial-temporal trends emerge that provide boundary conditions for synthetic data algorithms:

Decadal Shifts in Cyclonogenesis: Prior to the 1990s, the Bay of Bengal accounted for almost all extreme cyclonic fatalities due to a lack of early warning radar. Post-2010 trends show a significant rise in Arabian Sea severe cyclonic storms (e.g., Tauktae, Biparjoy) correlated with sea surface temperature (SST) warming.

Seismic Cycles along the Himalayan Boundary: The 1950 Assam-Tibet M8.6 earthquake established a high-magnitude benchmark for tectonic strain release. Mid-century and modern seismic events cluster tightly along the Main Central Thrust (MCT) and Main Boundary Thrust (MBT).

Hydro-Climatological Droughts to Cloudbursts: Major pan-Indian droughts (1965, 1972, 1987) were strongly coupled with strong El Niño conditions. Modern hydro-meteorological risks have shifted toward high-intensity, short-duration convective cloudbursts, flash floods, and GLOFs due to high-altitude warming.

4. Guidelines for Synthetic Data Generation (SDG) & Feature Engineering

When expanding this 76-year historical dataset into a multi-thousand row synthetic dataset using Generative AI, CTGAN, or Copulas, the following mathematical schema and categorical rules must be preserved:

Decadal Non-Stationarity: Account for climate non-stationarity across the 1950–2026 period. Frequency multipliers for extreme urban floods and Arabian Sea cyclones should increase post-2000, while drought impact severity on mortality decreases post-1990 due to improved food security networks.

Multi-Variable Feature Vector Mapping: Include variables: Latitude, Longitude, Elevation (m), Distance_to_Coast (km), 24h_Precipitation_mm, ENSO_ONI_Index (-2.0 to +2.5), Seismic_Zone (II-V), SST_Anomaly_C, River_Basin_Slope_deg, and Max_Wind_Speed_kmh.

Conditional Boundary Logic: Ensure hard boundary rules: e.g., Earthquakes must NOT have precipitation correlation; GLOFs must be restricted to Elevation > 2,000m; Severe Cyclones must occur within coastal proximity (< 150 km).
